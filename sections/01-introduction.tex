\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Introduce the problem and explain why it matters. A strong introduction usually establishes the practical or scientific context, identifies the limitation in prior work, states the central idea, and previews the evidence supporting the claims.

A concise contribution list can be useful:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item We formulate the target problem and define the evaluation criteria.
  \item We propose \methodname, a method designed around the identified constraints.
  \item We evaluate the method across representative settings and report reproducible results.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of the paper presents background in \cref{sec:background}, the method in \cref{sec:method}, and the evaluation in \cref{sec:experiments}.
